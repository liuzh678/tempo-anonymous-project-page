TEMPO: Semantic-Action Decoupled RL Post-Training for Vision-Language-Action Models

A two-timescale reinforcement learning framework that adapts the semantic projection layer and action expert at different update frequencies.

Abstract

Different modules, different adaptation needs

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are commonly adapted to downstream manipulation tasks through supervised fine-tuning or online reinforcement learning post-training. Supervised training is prone to distribution mismatch, while existing reinforcement learning approaches typically apply a single update strategy to all trainable components despite their different functional roles.

TEMPO is a semantic-action decoupled, two-timescale reinforcement learning framework for pretrained VLA models. It keeps the pretrained vision-language backbone unchanged and adapts two components through dedicated optimization loops: the semantic projection layer and the low-level action expert. The projection layer is updated conservatively to maintain a stable action-conditioning representation, while the action expert is updated more frequently to incorporate control feedback from online interaction.

Experiments on the CALVIN benchmark and two real-world manipulation tasks evaluate long-horizon completion, component contributions, update-frequency choices, and online learning behavior.

Method

Semantic-action decoupled reinforcement learning

A shared stream of robot interaction supplies task rewards to two representation-specific TD3 loops. The semantic loop operates between the frozen vision-language representation and the action-conditioning representation, while the action loop maps that representation to executable action chunks.

01

Preserved backbone

The pretrained vision-language model remains unchanged during reinforcement learning.

02

Semantic adaptation

A dedicated loop updates the semantic projection layer in the representation space.

03

Action adaptation

A separate loop updates Action-LoRA parameters using executable action chunks and interaction feedback.

The comparison includes the pretrained FLOWER policy, a single-loop reinforcement learning variant, and recent long-horizon baselines. TEMPO records the strongest SR5 and average chain length in the reported comparison.

Component and frequency studies in the paper examine semantic-only, action-only, equal-frequency, and frequency-decoupled variants under the same evaluation protocol.

Real-World Experiments

Online adaptation for prerequisite-dependent manipulation

Two physical tasks require the policy to handle a drawer before interacting with the target object. Curves show evaluation reward during online training, and frame sequences compare the learned execution order with FLOWER-RL.
